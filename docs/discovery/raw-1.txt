🔥 1. YouTube Titles (Click Drivers)

Your title has one job: earn the click. Not explain everything.

What works:
Curiosity gaps
→ “I Tested 100 AI Agents… Here’s What Happened”
Clear outcome/value
→ “Make $1,000/month with This Simple AI Workflow”
Strong specificity
→ Numbers, timeframes, constraints
What kills performance:
Vague titles: “My Thoughts on AI”
Over-explaining: too long = less punch
No emotional trigger

👉 Rule of thumb:
If someone can fully understand the video without clicking, your title is weak.

🎯 2. Thumbnails (Scroll Stoppers)

Thumbnail ≠ decoration
Thumbnail = pattern interrupt

High-performing traits:
One clear subject (face, object, or concept)
Big contrast (dark vs light, simple background)
Emotion or tension (shock, confusion, curiosity)
Visual story (before/after, vs, transformation)
What to avoid:
Clutter (too many elements)
Tiny details (mobile users won’t see them)
Generic stock-style visuals

👉 Think:
Can someone understand this in 0.5 seconds while scrolling?

🧠 3. Thumbnail Text (Optional but Powerful)

Text is NOT mandatory—but when used well, it boosts CTR.

Best practices:
1–4 words max
Reinforce or contrast the title
Use bold, readable fonts
Strong patterns:
Shock: “THIS WORKED”
Conflict: “BIG MISTAKE”
Curiosity: “NO ONE TALKS ABOUT THIS”
Numbers: “$0 → $10K”
What to avoid:
Repeating the title word-for-word
Long sentences
Small, thin fonts
⚡ The Real Secret: Title + Thumbnail = One Idea

They are a pair, not separate pieces.

Example:

Title:
“I Tried 5 AI Business Ideas”

Thumbnail text:
“ONLY 1 WORKED”

→ Now there’s tension. That’s what gets clicks.
